# D02: action consequences and resource use

**Approved by Vanna on October 3, 2026. Our discussion is the entirety of D02.** No implementation has occurred.

## Approved decision

The system must check that an action follows the author's established facts and rules. The author can provide these facts separately from the readable manuscript.

1. **State what an action requires and changes.** An action must declare its required effects and the effects it is allowed to have. Reject an event that omits a required change, changes the wrong person or object, adds an unsupported effect, or gives the wrong resulting amount. Use the existing event-generated assertions to record the changes.
2. **Ask when necessary information is missing.** Before an object is used, check the facts needed for that use. If its relevant attributes, starting amount, prior use or required condition are unknown, ask the author to clarify. Do not guess from the action or silently supply a value. Ask only for information relevant to the use.
3. **Allow clarification to establish a reusable rule.** An author can answer with a rule such as “All guns only have 12 rounds.” Store the author's answer and its agreed meaning for the stated world or universe, and apply it to the matching objects. The author need not repeat the same rule for every object. Clarify ambiguous meaning instead of choosing it silently.
4. **Carry resource use forward.** A twelve-round limit means each gun cannot fire more than twelve one-round shots between established reloads. A gun known to start loaded with twelve has zero after twelve shots; the thirteenth requires reloading. If its current load or earlier use is unknown, ask the author. A maximum alone does not prove that it is loaded. An explicit rule that every gun starts loaded with twelve can supply that initial amount once for each gun at its established start.
5. **Keep the history on the correct object.** Ammunition belongs to the gun being used. Changing its holder, mentioning it again, moving to another scene or restarting the system does not reset its ammunition. Reloading must be established with its amount; if the ammunition supply is constrained, account for that supply too. Two actions cannot both spend the same last round. Actions sharing a resource need distinct story times so the remaining amount is clear.
6. **Do not invent other attributes or consequences.** Walking across a battlefield does not establish immunity, safety or protection. It also does not justify inventing an injury. Apply relevant author-established rules for the world; do not import rules from another world or assume terrestrial physics without support.
7. **Protect continuity when rules change.** Keep old records readable and unchanged. Editing an old action or event requires the relevant declarations. Changing an action's rules requires the author's approval and rechecks the events that use those rules. A particular object cannot silently bypass an applicable world rule. An exception or change must be explicit and authorized. Earlier validation cannot approve changed facts or rules, and earlier editions retain their original history.

This approves the behavior above. It does not approve general simulation, changes to unrelated features, or additional requirements hidden in the earlier technical proposal. Existing authority and publication protections continue to apply. A demonstrated design flaw must be brought to Vanna plainly before changing the approved behavior.

## Approval record

Vanna first agreed that relevant object attributes and action effects must be explicit, then required author clarification and reusable world/universe rules with cumulative resource accounting. Vanna's final approval was: “As long as our discussion is the totallity of D02, then it's approved.” This document records that boundary; it does not treat approval of behavior as approval of every earlier implementation suggestion.
